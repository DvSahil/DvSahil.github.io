The big picture

How a robot works

Every robot, from a factory arm to a Mars rover, repeats one loop: sense, think, act. It then senses again to check the result. This is called feedback and runs many times per second.

1 · Sense
Sensors turn the world into numbers: encoders give joint angles, an IMU gives tilt, LiDAR gives distance, the DHT22 gives temperature and humidity.
2 · Think
A controller (Arduino, ESP32 or a PC running ROS) compares what it senses with what it wants, finds the error and chooses the next command.
3 · Act
Actuators turn commands into motion: motors turn the joints, hydraulic cylinders push heavy links, the gripper opens and closes.

Twelve joint types: freedom allowed and removed

A free body has 6 degrees of freedom. A joint that allows f of them removes 6 − f.

Five common arm layouts

Letters show joint order from the base: R is revolute (turns), P is prismatic (slides).

Chapter 1 · Basics

Links, joints and an end effector

Robots come in two big families: manipulators (arms that handle objects: industrial arms, cobots, medical robots) and mobile robots (moving on wheels, legs or propellers). A robot arm is a chain: rigid links connected by joints, with an end effector (a gripper or tool) at the tip. Tap any label on the 3D arm.

Types of links

Rigid link
No deformation while transmitting motion: the links of an industrial arm.
Flexible link
Deforms partly: a belt drive.
Fluid link
Motion by fluid pressure: hydraulic actuators.

Chapter 2 · Degrees of freedom

How many ways can it move?

A free object in space has 6 degrees of freedom: 3 translations (x, y, z) and 3 rotations (roll, pitch, yaw). Every joint takes some freedoms away and leaves one or more. A human arm has 7: shoulder 3, elbow 1, wrist 3. Wiggle each joint of this arm:

Chapter 3 · Mathematics of motion

Describing orientation with matrices

To compute where the gripper is, we attach a coordinate frame to every part. A rotation matrix describes how one frame is turned relative to another; adding a translation gives a 4 × 4 homogeneous transform. Turn the 3D body with the sliders: the matrix updates live.

R = Rx · Ry · Rz    T = [ R t ; 0 0 0 1 ]

Multiplying matrices in order is how robots chain frames: turn about Z first, then Y, then X (about the fixed axes). Order matters: rotating about X then Y is not the same as Y then X.

Chapter 4 · Forward kinematics & DH

Chapter 5 · Inverse kinematics

Where do I put the joints to reach a point?

The reverse problem is what programmers actually need: I want the gripper here, what are the joint angles? For a two-link arm there is a closed-form answer, and usually two solutions (elbow up and elbow down). Tap or drag in the plane below; the 3D arm follows.

cos θ₃ = (x² + z² − L₁² − L₂²) / (2·L₁·L₂)
θ₂ = atan2(z, x) − atan2(L₂ sin θ₃, L₁ + L₂ cos θ₃)

Outside the dashed circle the point cannot be reached; very close to the base, the arm cannot fold that tightly either. Near full stretch the arm loses freedom (a singularity).

Calculations

Kinematics worked by hand

Kinematics studies motion without asking which force causes it. Here is a 2-link arm solved step by step. Change the numbers and watch every line recalculate. The defaults are link lengths from my DH table (240 mm and 133.6 mm).

x = L₁·cosθ₁ + L₂·cos(θ₁+θ₂)   y = L₁·sinθ₁ + L₂·sin(θ₁+θ₂)
cosθ₂ = (x² + y² − L₁² − L₂²) / (2·L₁·L₂)

Flipping the sign of θ₂ gives the second "elbow" solution, which is why inverse kinematics can have two answers. If the target is farther than L₁ + L₂, there is no answer at all.

Rules that rotation matrices obey

Order matters
Rx·Ry·Rz is not the same as Rz·Ry·Rx. Turn a book 90° about two axes in two different orders and it ends up facing differently.
Inverse = transpose
For a rotation matrix R, R⁻¹ equals Rᵀ, so undoing a rotation is cheap. Its determinant is 1 and every column has length 1.
Chaining frames
The hand pose in the base frame is T = A₁·A₂·A₃·A₄. Each matrix moves you from one joint frame to the next: one multiplication per joint.

For Rx(45°)·Ry(30°)·Rz(60°) the rotation part is about [0.433 −0.750 0.500; 0.789 0.047 −0.612; 0.436 0.660 0.612]. The Rotations chapter computes it live.

Chapter 7 · Embedded integration

The robot's nervous system

A microcontroller (Arduino) reads sensors (inputs), decides, and drives actuators (outputs): the loop at the heart of every robot. On the 3D bench: an Arduino Uno, a hobby servo and an ultrasonic distance sensor. ESP32 boards work the same way and add Wi-Fi for connected projects.

Servo: angle becomes a pulse width

A servo expects a pulse every 20 ms. Pulse length (about 1 ms to 2 ms) sets its angle.

Ultrasonic sensor: echo time becomes distance

The sensor sends a 40 kHz ping and times the echo. Distance = (time × speed of sound) ÷ 2.

Hardware
ESP32, DHT22 and the parts around them


My electronics task used an ESP32 board with a DHT22 temperature and humidity sensor, programmed with the Arduino software.


ESP32
A cheap, small microcontroller board with built-in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, popular for smart-home gadgets and weather stations.
DHT22
Senses temperature and humidity and sends them as a digital signal, about one new reading every 2 seconds.
The program
Name the pin, start the sensor, wait, read both values and print them. If a read fails, print an error.


Try the sensor
Serial monitor 115200 baud
Unplug the sensor to see the error handling.


#include "DHT.h"
#define DHT_PIN 4
#define DHT_TYPE DHT22
DHT dht(DHT_PIN, DHT_TYPE);

void setup() { Serial.begin(115200); dht.begin(); }

void loop() {
  delay(2000);                       // let the reading settle
  float t = dht.readTemperature();
  float h = dht.readHumidity();
  if (isnan(t) || isnan(h)) { Serial.println("Failed to read from DHT sensor!"); return; }
  Serial.print("Temperature: "); Serial.print(t);
  Serial.print(" C  Humidity: "); Serial.print(h); Serial.println(" %");
}

Sensors a robot uses


PWM
Pulse-width modulation switches power on and off very fast. A longer "on" share means a faster motor or a brighter LED, so a digital pin can control power.
Torque and gearing
A gearbox trades speed for torque. 100:1 gives about 100 times the torque at 1/100 of the speed, which is why arm joints use geared motors.






Chapter 8 · Control
Making a joint go exactly where you say


Open-loop control just sends a command and hopes. Closed-loop control measures the real angle with a sensor, compares it to the target, and corrects. The most common recipe is PID: P pushes in proportion to the error, I removes leftover error, D damps overshoot. Tune a joint that must swing 60°, then press Play to watch the 3D arm follow.

Chapter 9 · Robot Operating System
How the robot's software talks to itself


ROS is not an operating system but a framework. A robot's software is split into small programs called nodes. Nodes exchange data by publishing messages to named topics, and by subscribing to topics. ROS commands I used and why
A ROS 1 system needs a master, started with roscore (roslaunch starts one for you). ROS 2 uses the same ideas with commands such as ros2 topic echo and needs no master.


URDF
An XML file describing links, joints and sensors of the robot model.
Gazebo
A physics simulator: test code on a virtual rover before the real one.
Packages & workspace
Folders that organise code; built with catkin_make in ROS 1.
Sensor topics
LiDAR, camera or IMU data arrives as topics you can echo or process.


Good to know
In 2023, ROS 1 (Noetic) was standard; its support ended on 31 May 2025. New projects use ROS 2 (for example Jazzy Jalisco, supported to 2029), with the same ideas of nodes, topics and messages but new tools.






Chapter 10 · Putting it together
An automated pick-and-place cycle


Automation chains everything: inverse kinematics turns each target position into joint angles, motion control moves the joints smoothly, and the gripper (an actuator) closes on the object. Press Run and the arm picks the yellow block and places it on the other mark, over and over, just as a factory cell would.




Behind the scenes it is a state machine: each state runs until its goal is reached, then the next begins.
