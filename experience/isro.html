Why send robots instead of people?

Space is too cold, too hot, too far and too dangerous. Robots go first. Pick a destination to see what they face.

Why delay matters

A command takes time to reach the robot and the picture takes time to come back, so a human cannot steer in real time. Press the button to send a command.

Rover lab: drive it like Pragyan

India's Chandrayaan-3 rover, Pragyan, has six wheels, each with its own motor, and steers by spinning the left and right sides at different speeds. It has no steering wheel. Try it.

Drag to orbit · the camera follows the rover
Both sides equal: straight line. Different: it curves. Opposite: it spins on the spot.

The real Pragyan moves at about 1 cm per second. This demo is much faster so you can see the effect.

Rocker-bogie suspension

Two L-shaped linkages let each wheel rise or fall without tilting the body much. Pragyan's has about 50 mm of travel.

Six independent motors

Each wheel is driven by its own brushless DC motor, so the rover keeps moving if one wheel struggles.

Self-driving: how a rover avoids rocks

NASA's Perseverance rover scores possible paths ahead of it and picks a safe one. This is a simplified version of that idea. Green paths are safe, red paths hit a rock.

AutoNav: every moment the rover tests nine curved paths, throws out the unsafe ones, and takes the safe one that gets closest to the goal.

The hard parts of space robotics

Temperature

Swings of hundreds of degrees. Parts expand, contract and can lose calibration.

Radiation

Electronics can suffer bit flips or damage, so they need shielding and fault-tolerant design.

Dust

Lunar dust is abrasive and clingy. It jams joints, wears surfaces and degrades cameras.

Delay

Seconds to the Moon, minutes to Mars. Robots need autonomy to act between commands.

Power and the long night

A lunar night lasts about 14 Earth days. Pragyan was designed for one lunar day of work.

No GPS

Rovers navigate with cameras and by comparing images as they move (visual odometry).

Real missions behind the ideas

Pragyan, Chandrayaan-3 (ISRO)

Landed near the lunar south pole on 23 August 2023. About 26 to 27 kg, six wheels, rocker-bogie suspension, planned to travel 500 m at 1 cm per second.

Perseverance AutoNav (NASA)

Builds 3D maps from its cameras, finds hazards and plans around them. Called "thinking while driving". It can reach 393 feet per hour.
