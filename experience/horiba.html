The 60-second version

The problem

Every new vehicle must prove its exhaust is clean before it can be sold. Labs need instruments they can trust to the last decimal.

The idea

A treadmill for cars plus a breathalyzer for exhaust: the car "drives" a fixed route on rollers indoors while analyzers measure what leaves the tailpipe.

Why it matters

Results decide if a car meets Indian BS-VI rules (AIS 137). Cleaner air, and manufacturers can legally launch the model.

The company: HORIBA

Where I spent the four weeks: the Energy & Environment department at HORIBA India, HITC Pune, guided by Mr. Avinash Shinde (Senior Manager).

  • Origin: founded in 1945 by Masao Horiba in Kyoto, Japan, starting with pH meters.
  • Today: a global precision-instrument company with 8,000+ employees across five segments: Automotive, Environmental, Medical, Semiconductor and Scientific.
  • Motto: "Joy and Fun": enjoy solving problems and build things that make society cleaner and safer.
  • In India: supplies turnkey emission-testing labs (analyzers, dynamometers, automation software, post-processing) so manufacturers can meet BS-VI under AIS 137.

How to read the layout diagram

  1. Car on rollers (left): driven wheels turn the dynamometer, which also measures force.
  2. Exhaust flow meter: records how much exhaust the engine produces.
  3. Dilution tunnel (CVS) (the big pipe along the top): mixes exhaust with air; a blower on the right keeps the flow constant.
  4. MEXA-ONE-C1 cabinets (right): read CO, CO₂, NOx, THC and CH₄ from the diluted sample.
  5. Side instruments: particulate mass (PM) and particle-number (PN) counters, plus THC/NO units. Newer Euro 6d / Euro 7 set-ups add an ammonia (NH₃) analyzer, weather station and driver's aid.
  6. Operation area: the engineer runs the test from the STARS automation software.

2 · How the analyzer "smells" exhaust

MEXA = Motor Exhaust Gas Analyzer, HORIBA's family of exhaust-measuring instruments. Instead of one sensor, it uses a different physical trick for each gas. Pick one:

Meet the MEXA family

MEXA-ONE: the full lab system

Modular and configurable, with versions for different jobs:

  • D1: measures raw exhaust directly
  • C1: measures diluted exhaust from a CVS
  • DC: dual mode, direct and diluted
  • D2: dual-line direct measurement

Used for both light-duty and heavy-duty vehicle testing, and controlled by a touchscreen showing live gas levels, calibration mode, drift and alerts.

MEXA-CUBE: the compact one

Same measuring principles scaled down: compact GC/FID, miniaturised CLD, simple interface with USB/Ethernet. Ideal for research labs, mobile set-ups and quick roadside measurements.

Safe shutdown

Before power-off, a purge with clean air or nitrogen brings THC below 10 ppmC. This prevents contamination and damage to the detectors.

Built-in pre-alarms

Warns about calibration drift, pump/line pressure differences, heater faults and converter efficiency falling below 95%, so problems are fixed before they spoil a test.

3 · A vehicle test, step by step

Soak the car6+ hours at 20–25 °C. Why: a cold start is the hardest case for emissions, so every test must begin identically.
Mount on the rollersFront-, rear- or all-wheel drive decides which axle sits on the dyno. Why: only driven wheels turn the rollers.
Calibrate everythingDyno with 14 kg certified weights; analyzers with zero gas (nitrogen/zero air) and span gas. Why: an uncalibrated instrument makes the result legally worthless.
Pick a driving cycleIDC, MIDC or WLTC: a scripted speed-versus-time route mixing city and highway phases. Why: same route for every car makes results comparable.
Drive & sampleExhaust is diluted in the CVS and sent to the analyzers; lambda (air-fuel ratio), RPM, speed and fuel use are logged together. Alarms halt the test if readings drift.
ReportData is checked for exceedances, sometimes cross-verified against AVL equipment, and compiled into a certification report.

4 · Engine bench: the car without the car

Here only the engine is mounted on a dynamometer, with its own fuel, coolant and sensors. Why separate? Engineers can control conditions precisely, tune the engine, and test the exhaust after-treatment on its own. Exhaust flows through:

Engine→After-treatment (DOC/DPF/SCR)→MEXA D1 (engine-out)→MEXA D2 (post-treatment)→Opacity · MDLT (particulates) · FTIR (NH₃)
  • D1 vs D2 shows how much pollution the after-treatment removes.
  • Opacity meter measures smoke; MDLT measures particulate matter; FTIR reads ammonia.
  • Fuel lines are colour-coded: orange for petrol, blue for diesel.
  • Procedures follow AIS 137 Part 4: pre-conditioning, calibration, engine mapping, steady-state and transient cycles, deterioration factors. Example load: Cummins generator engines.

The real engine bench

What is on the bench
  • Mounting frame: holds the engine rigidly, with vibration damping
  • Dynamometer (eddy-current or AC): applies load, measures torque and RPM
  • Fuel flow meter + regulator: live fuel use at controlled temperature and pressure
  • Cooling system: manages coolant temperature and flow
  • CAHU: keeps intake-air pressure and temperature steady
  • Boom box: sensor hub for pressure and fuel flow

The measurement layout

Raw exhaust is sampled for gases (THC, CH₄, NOx, CO, CO₂, HCHO), ammonia and N₂O, solid particle number (SPN10 and SPN23) and particulate mass. Before a run, pre-checks verify sensors, test for leaks in fuel, exhaust and coolant lines, and rehearse emergency shut-off. Engines up to 180 kW or 380 kW can be tested.

Glossary

  • Dynamometer (dyno): a device that applies and measures load and speed.
  • g/km: grams of pollutant per kilometre driven, the unit of legal limits.
  • ppm: parts per million, a gas concentration.
  • Lambda: air-fuel ratio; 1 is the ideal mix.
  • Zero / span gas: reference gases with none / a known amount of the target gas.
  • RDE / PEMS: real-road emissions testing using portable instruments.
